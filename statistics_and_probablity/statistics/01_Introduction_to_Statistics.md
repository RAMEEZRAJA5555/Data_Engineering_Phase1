# Descriptive Statistics

## 1. Definition

Descriptive Statistics is a branch of statistics that deals with collecting, organizing, summarizing, analyzing, and presenting data in a meaningful way.

**In simple words:** Descriptive Statistics helps us understand and describe the data we already have.

**Example:**

Suppose the marks of five students are:

`60, 70, 80, 90, 100`

Using descriptive statistics, we can:
- Calculate the average marks.
- Find the highest and lowest marks.
- Identify the most frequent marks.
- Present the marks using tables and graphs.

## 2. Purpose of Descriptive Statistics

The main purposes are:

1. To organize raw data.
2. To summarize large datasets.
3. To find important information from data.
4. To identify patterns and trends.
5. To make data easier to understand.
6. To present information using tables, charts, and graphs.

## 3. Main Methods of Descriptive Statistics

Descriptive Statistics mainly uses three methods:

### A. Measures of Central Tendency

**Definition:** Measures of Central Tendency are statistical methods used to find the central or typical value of a dataset.

They tell us where the center of the data is located.

The three main measures are:

- **Mean:** The average value of a dataset.
- **Median:** The middle value after arranging the data.
- **Mode:** The most frequently occurring value.

**Example:**

Data: `10, 20, 20, 30, 40`

- Mean = 24
- Median = 20
- Mode = 20

### B. Measures of Dispersion

**Definition:** Measures of Dispersion are statistical methods used to understand how spread out or scattered the data values are.

They tell us how much the values differ from one another.

Common measures include:

- **Range:** Difference between the maximum and minimum values.
- **Variance:** Measures the average squared spread around the mean.
- **Standard Deviation:** Measures how far values typically spread from the mean.

**Example:**

Dataset A: `10, 20, 30`

Dataset B: `19, 20, 21`

Both datasets have the same mean (20), but Dataset A has more spread.

### C. Graphical Representation of Data

**Definition:** Graphical Representation means presenting data using visual forms such as charts and graphs.

It makes data easier to understand and compare.

Common graphical methods include:

- **Bar Chart:** Compares different categories.
- **Histogram:** Shows the frequency distribution of numerical data.
- **Pie Chart:** Shows proportions of a whole.
- **Line Graph:** Shows changes or trends over time.
- **Box Plot:** Displays the distribution, median, quartiles, and possible outliers.

## 4. Characteristics of Descriptive Statistics

1. It summarizes existing data.
2. It organizes raw information.
3. It uses numerical calculations.
4. It uses tables, charts, and graphs.
5. It helps identify patterns in data.
6. It does not automatically make conclusions about an entire population from a sample.

## 5. Example of Descriptive Statistics

Suppose the following dataset represents the marks of five students:

`50, 60, 70, 80, 90`

We can describe this dataset:

| Measure | Result |
|---|---:|
| Number of students | 5 |
| Minimum marks | 50 |
| Maximum marks | 90 |
| Mean | 70 |
| Median | 70 |
| Range | 40 |

This information gives us a summary of the students' marks.

## 6. Applications of Descriptive Statistics

- **Education:** Summarizing students' marks and calculating class averages.
- **Business:** Summarizing monthly sales and profits.
- **Healthcare:** Summarizing patient information.
- **Sports:** Analyzing players' performance.
- **Data Science:** Understanding datasets before building machine learning models.

## 7. Important Summary

- Descriptive Statistics summarizes and describes existing data.
- It helps organize and understand large datasets.
- Its three main methods are central tendency, dispersion, and graphical representation.
- Mean, median, and mode describe the center of data.
- Range, variance, and standard deviation describe the spread of data.
- Charts and graphs present data visually.
- It describes data rather than making population-wide predictions from a sample.

## 8. Important Difference

| Descriptive Statistics | Inferential Statistics |
|---|---|
| Describes available data. | Draws conclusions about a population. |
| Summarizes information. | Makes estimates and predictions. |
| Uses mean, median, mode, and graphs. | Uses sampling, probability, and statistical tests. |
| Focuses on the observed dataset. | Uses sample data to study a population. |
